When You Can't Borrow His Faith

Your husband's faith cannot carry you through every storm. Here is how I learned to stand on my own faith when everything was on the line.

Adapted from Dear Pastor's Wife by May Ijisesan.

It was around noon when my phone rang. My husband was in Africa on a mission trip, about five days into a two-week journey, with a major miracle crusade scheduled for that very evening. The voice on the line was my son's school: there had been an accident. He had fallen, hit his head on the pavement, and seemed disoriented.

When I reached him, my fifth-grader was sitting in a corner, and my heart sank as he looked up and asked, "Mummy, where am I?" Then, "What day is it?" "Where is daddy?" He had a concussion and had lost his short-term memory. Every five minutes he asked the same questions, and every five minutes I answered: Your birthday is March 16th. You are eleven. Daddy is in Africa.

I wanted to call my husband more than anything. But the crusade was starting in about thirty minutes, and hundreds of people were expecting a move of God. As anointed as my husband is, he is still a man, that news would have shattered his focus. I was not willing to sacrifice my son. But I was ready to reach for the other resources God had given me. On the way to the emergency room, between speaking in tongues, I kept patting my son's leg: "You will be fine. We took communion this morning, and the blood of Jesus guarantees your safety." I called an associate pastor to stand with me in prayer. Within a day, my son's memory fully returned.

That day taught me something I want you to carry: first-response matters in the fight of faith. And every pastor's wife must have a faith of her own.

You cannot live on someone else's faith

There will be challenges that require your faith to overcome, moments when the person you would normally lean on cannot be reached, or cannot be told. Your husband brings his faith to the table, and thank God for it. But it would not serve your family for him to bring faith while you bring fear and worry. That combination drags a household down instead of lifting it.

Understand this too: the enemy does not oppose you merely because you are a Christian. He opposes you because you are in ministry, married to someone committed to bringing light into dark places. You are a threat to him, and he knows it. But you do not have to be afraid of his schemes, because you have been given world-overcoming faith. Even faith the size of a mustard seed accomplishes great things.

Have the God-kind of faith

Scripture tells us to "have the faith of God", the God-kind of faith. That means using your faith the way God uses His: calling things that do not yet exist into existence. If there is turmoil, speak peace. If there is lack, speak provision. If there is sickness, speak health. Your faith is a weapon that works at all times.

Remember Daniel in the lions' den. He walked in seemingly without a weapon, but he carried faith greater than anything the eye could see. He looked smaller than his situation and was, in fact, orders of magnitude greater in the spirit. So are you. Before the concussion ever came for my son, we had already overcome it; I simply had to speak and act in line with a victory that was already mine.

Ignore the contrary report, stay in praise

Abraham is our picture of a faith that holds. He expected something different from what he could see. Naturally, he had no hope of a child at his age, but in God's Word he had a different hope, and he refused to factor his own failing body into the equation. Some circumstances will speak loudly against what God has said. Learn to ignore them. If you do not learn to handle what the facts are shouting, the facts will handle you.

And notice what else Abraham did: he stayed in praise and thanksgiving. Praise keeps the atmosphere of heaven around you so your faith can bring the promise into view. Murmuring does the opposite, it gives the enemy room to work. One more thing that frees me every time I remember it: Abraham was not a perfect man. He lied about his wife, more than once. Yet God honored his faith. You do not have to be perfect for God to respond to your faith.

Your faith empowers you

Years ago I wanted us to move from an old rented townhouse in Skokie into a home with room for my growing children. My husband did not see the problem, so at first he did not engage. And then it dawned on me, and it was so freeing: I did not need my husband's permission to believe God for a new place. I had God's permission in His Word. God is no respecter of persons; if He honored Abraham's faith, He would honor mine. So instead of fighting about it, I went to the Word and meditated on His promises for months. Eventually my husband saw it too (with the eyes of the spirit) and he took the lead and carried us to the finish line. God gave us that house in a miraculous way.

Sometimes you do not start with a conversation. You start with the Word. The just shall live by her own faith.

Reflect

What is one thing God has said in His Word that your current circumstances are contradicting? Write the promise down. Then, instead of rehearsing the problem today, rehearse the promise, out loud, with thanksgiving.

May Ijisesan
