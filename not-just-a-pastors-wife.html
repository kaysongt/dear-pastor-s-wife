You Are Not "Just a Pastor's Wife"

The label "pastor's wife" was never meant to shrink you. Discover the two-fold call you've been doubly graced to carry.

Adapted from Dear Pastor's Wife by May Ijisesan.

Years ago, I invited a woman to my office for our first real conversation. Back then I ran a Business Intelligence consulting company, and I had built a workspace I was proud of — the kind of room that inspired me to work. She had known me only from church. When she walked in and took her seat, she looked around and said, "Wow, Pastor May, I never knew this part of you. I thought you were just a pastor's wife."

Just a pastor's wife.

That phrase stayed with me. I turned it over for months, especially later when God asked me to close that business. As long as I had the company, I could tell myself I was more than "just a pastor's wife." But once it was gone, the words began to haunt me. Would people overlook me? Would I become invisible, blending into the background of my husband's more colorful ministry life? What would become of my dreams, my gifts, my interests?

If you have ever felt frustrated by a label that does not accurately describe you, this is for you.

"Pastor's wife" is a title, not a five-fold ministry gift

One day, God told me something that reordered how I saw my life: "Pastor's wife" is not a five-fold ministry gift. There is no calling called "pastor's wife." I could not simply wear the title, wave it around, and assume I had fulfilled my purpose. One day I will stand before God and give an account of everything He deposited in me — not just how I supported my husband, but what I did with my own gifts and grace.

That is why I like to say you have a two-fold call, and you are doubly graced for it. There is the assignment to help, and there is the unique purpose God formed you for before you were born. The title you gained through marriage does not cancel the person God made you to be.

Help is not a weak word

Somewhere along the way, we absorbed the idea that being a "helper" means being lesser. Let me put that to rest. The Holy Spirit is called our Helper, and He is neither weak nor subservient. He is God, dwelling in us to help us. When someone is called to help another, it does not make them inferior.

I once heard about a newly elected mayor who stopped for gas with his wife on the way home from his inauguration. The attendant turned out to be his wife's high school boyfriend. As they drove off, the mayor teased, "If you'd married him, you'd be the wife of a gas attendant." His wife replied, "No — if I'd married him, he would be mayor, and God knows what you'd be." That is a woman who understood her design. She was created to help a man become something.

Your contribution is real. Over the years, my husband has benefited from my creativity and ideas that reshaped our ministry. There were seasons my income carried our home while he answered God's call. It is not pride to acknowledge what you bring to the table; it is stewardship. You cannot be effective if you refuse to see your own worth.

Discover your purpose and discover yourself

Two pursuits have to run side by side. The first is purpose-discovery — knowing why you exist, the problem you were sent to solve. The second is self-discovery — knowing the tools God deposited in you to solve it.

Think of a toolbox. When I need to hang a shelf, I reach past everything else for the hammer and screwdriver, because a tool has to match its purpose. But it is not enough to know the hammer is the right tool; I need to understand its features — that the blunt head drives the nail, not the pronged end. You are the tool. You also need to study your own features.

Here is why both matter. Self-discovery without purpose leads to selfishness — you get so enamored with your gifts that you spend them only on yourself. Purpose without self-discovery leads to intimidation — you sense the assignment but feel unequipped, so you shrink back. God will always equip you for what He has called you to do. If you feel overwhelmed, it may mean you are attempting something He did not assign, or you have gifts still waiting to be developed.

So stop despising your peculiarities. The very things that once made you feel like the odd one out are often the strengths hidden inside your assignment. Everyone else is taken. Be yourself.

Occupy the space God gave you

You are not a plus-one. You are not an afterthought who happened to come with the offer. When God surveyed creation, He decided the picture was not complete without the woman — He would not sign off on His work until He had made you. That is how essential you are.

So hold your head up. Do not let people redefine you when God has already defined you. Reach into your core, reach up to Him, and give an account one day of a life that occupied every inch of the space grace made for you.

Reflect

Set the title aside for a moment. What has God deposited in you — a gift, an idea, a burden that will not leave — that has nothing to do with being a pastor's wife? Write it down. What is one step you could take this month to develop it?

May Ijisesan
