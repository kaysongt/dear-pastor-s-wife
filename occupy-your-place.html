Purpose & Legacy

Occupy Your Place: How to Stop Existing and Start Thriving in Your Calling

Purpose is not discovered in comfort. Here is how I found mine one assignment at a time, and how you can occupy the place God made for you.

Adapted from Dear Pastor's Wife by May Ijisesan.

When I was a teenager, I attended a fellowship where they handed out a form asking what I believed my calling was. I left the box blank. I genuinely had no idea. I knew I would be a pastor's wife (God had told me that much) but beyond that, the page was empty.

If your box still feels blank, take heart. Purpose is rarely handed to you all at once. It is discovered in the journey.

You discover purpose one assignment at a time

For me, purpose has always been tied to needs and assignments. Either God would ask me to do something and I would find there was more to it than met the eye, or I would see a need, meet it, and realize the process itself connected to something deeper in me.

The first time I taught at church was on a Mother's Day, only because my husband asked me to. I was so nervous I needed every one of the three weeks he gave me to prepare. But when it was over, I realized I loved it. Interestingly, the topic that day was "Purpose." I did not begin knowing I was a teacher. I began by saying yes.

My women's conferences started the same way. The very first one hardly looked like a conference, I invited every woman in the church and only a handful showed up. We prayed, I shared the Word, and we ate. Looking back, I am so glad I did not let the size of the room discourage me. From that small gathering grew a "conference train" that now moves between London, Lagos, and Chicago. I never set out to be a conference host. I saw a need and tried to meet it.

You will not find purpose in comfort

I have to tell you something a family member once said that stung, but freed me. After that first purpose message, I found it so comfortable to preach that I taught it every single time I got up to speak. One day my brother asked, "Don't you have any other message? You preach the same thing every time." He was right. I had found a safe zone and pitched my tent there.

His words jolted me out of complacency. I began to stretch into other topics, and it was uncomfortable, but that is exactly the point. You will not discover your purpose in the place of comfort and convenience. Your purpose is in God; you have to reach up to Him and reach into your core to find it. Over time I learned that the strength of my message was in its relatability, and relatability takes vulnerability. It is not always easy to be vulnerable, but people learn, sometimes even at my expense.

Flexibility is not forfeiting your dreams

Here is where many women lose ground. The moment they meet resistance (an obstacle, a scheduling conflict, a season that demands their attention elsewhere) they abandon their dreams entirely. Please do not do that.

The word helpmeet carries a flexibility component; a helpmeet is a suitable and adaptable help. My husband has assignments the Lord has given him, and so do I, and both are urgent. So I choose flexibility over rigidity. If I communicate what I sense God leading me to do, we can usually find a way to work around each other's schedules. Sometimes I have had to release a plan for a season, and often God worked it out at a better time. Other times I simply trusted He would use someone else. But letting a dream breathe is very different from letting it die. Quite often we just need creativity and wisdom to get things done. Your dreams and visions do matter.

Occupy your place, don't just occupy space

Several years ago, the Lord gave me a conference theme: Occupy Your Place. I did not fully understand it at first. When I finally did, I realized God wanted women to be fully present, in the home, in ministry, in business, in career, in society. Not merely occupying space, but occupying their place.

Think of the bathroom on a long flight. You feel the urge, you hurry down the aisle, and you hope the sign reads vacant. How frustrating when it reads occupied, someone is already there; there is no room for another. In the same way, when people, circumstances, or the enemy try to move into the space assigned to your life and destiny, make sure that when they arrive, the sign reads occupied. Because if you do not fill your place, something or someone else will try to.

You cannot fulfill your purpose alone, and you will not discover all of it in one season. But you can keep obeying, keep meeting the needs in front of you, and keep stepping, until your world knows you were here.

Reflect

Name one "place" God has spoken to your heart about, a room, a role, a need only you seem to see. What is the smallest brave step you could take this week to move from occupying space to occupying your place?

May Ijisesan
