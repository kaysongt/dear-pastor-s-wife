Guarded and Gracious: Loving Your Church Fully Without Losing Yourself

Ministry is about people, and people can hurt. Here's how to build rich church relationships, set wise boundaries, and heal a heart that's closed up.

Adapted from Dear Pastor's Wife by May Ijisesan.

There was a season when a group of young ladies I had poured years into walked out of my life in quick succession. Some I had taken as family. I had invested time, money, and love in them. When they left, something in me quietly shut down.

So I made a subconscious decision to be more guarded. I stayed friendly, but I never fully opened my heart again, I was protecting it from another heartbreak. What I did not realize is that the heart is a two-way street. When you guard it to keep hurt out, you also keep love from flowing out. That is the tension every pastor's wife lives in: ministry is about people, and people are exactly what can wound us. Let me share what I have learned about loving well without losing yourself.

Build relationship equity

Relationships are made of giving and taking. When you invest (an unsolicited kindness, genuine care, showing up for someone) you make a deposit. When you call in favors or lean on someone's resources, you make a withdrawal. Relationship equity means you are consistently investing more than you withdraw. That is simply God's love in action.

This does not mean you never receive. For years I have "adopted" bachelors in the church, showing up with home-cooked meals in Tupperware, not to get anything back, but because being kind is good practice. And I have never been shy about asking a young woman I was mentoring to help with my daughter's hair or a grocery run, because I knew I was pouring something of eternal value into her at the same time. Do not be so self-sufficient that you deny your members the blessing of honoring you. Just be careful never to make people feel used. Remember: every member of your church today is a potential ex-member. If someone ever leaves, let it not be because you mismanaged the relationship.

You can be guarded and gracious

Hospitality is central to who we are as pastors' wives, sharing our homes, our food, our resources. But hospitality must be paired with wisdom. You cannot open your door to just anyone. When someone comes to live with your family, study them, with your physical and your spiritual eyes, and let that determine the level of access they receive. The fact that someone lives with you does not entitle them to unfettered access to your family. Being guarded does not mean being cold; you can be guarded and gracious at the same time.

Set house rules and make them non-negotiable. If a living arrangement is compromising your family, you have every right to change it, and you should not feel guilty about it. I know a pastor's wife who eventually had to ask a houseguest to leave because, despite repeated conversations, she would not stop dressing inappropriately in their home. They still wanted to help her, they simply had to do it from outside their home. Protecting your family is not a failure of love. It is stewardship.

Anointed and flawed

I chose those words carefully, anointed and flawed, not anointed but flawed. Apart from Jesus, every single person in your church is both at once. The closer you get to people, the more of their imperfections you will see, and some may genuinely put you off. When that happens, remind yourself that a person's flaws do not diminish the anointing on their life. David honored Saul as the Lord's anointed even after Saul had been rejected as king. Believe the best of people while acknowledging they are flawed, so when the flaw surfaces, you are neither shocked nor devastated. (There is a limit: when a flaw becomes an unrepentant, harmful pattern, love may require keeping a wise distance. But that is the exception, not the posture.)

Let the church family bless you, and let hurt go

Do not stand aloof from your own church. When my mother passed after a long illness, I did not share the details with many people, but a smile here, a hug there, a word of encouragement at just the right moment carried me through and brought me out stronger. Do not deny yourself the blessing a church family can be.

And when you have been hurt, do the hard, holy work of letting go. A pastor's wife cannot simply leave and find another church the way a member can, she is as married to the church as she is to her husband. So the hurt has nowhere to go but inward, and too many of us are bleeding in silence. For every offense I clung to, the Lord showed me I was forfeiting a chance to minister to someone new. My deepest healing came at one of our women's retreats, when I became vulnerable in a breakout session and wept, and God began to reopen my heart. You can be busy in ministry and still have a closed heart. If your experiences have shut you down, I want to encourage you to open up again, not because people will stop being imperfect, but because there is a perfect God big enough to hold you when they fail you.

Reflect

Is there a hurt you are guarding, a heart you have quietly closed to protect yourself? Ask the Holy Spirit to show you what that closed door is also keeping out. Then pray for the courage to open, one relationship at a time.

May Ijisesan
